                       Recent developments in LLMs have made possible for autonomous software
                       engineering agents to do various activities such as code creation, repository
                       management, debugging, testing, and planning. Nevertheless, the dependability of
                       these autonomous agents is still hampered by issues associated with failure recovery,
                       adaptation, and learning from previous errors. This study offers an organized survey of
                       the current literature comprising 35 papers about autonomous software engineering
                       agents and specifically addresses issues associated with self-reflection, memory,
                       planning, repository-aware reasoning, multi-agent cooperation, benchmarking, and
                       failure recovery. The research identifies a number of research gaps in this area such
                       as the absence of comprehensive recovery frameworks, root-cause analysis, memory-
                       aided adaptive reasoning, recovery-based evaluation, and failure prevention. The
                       results indicate that there is a need for development of recovery techniques that would
                       allow autonomous software agents to recognize their failures, adjust their actions, and
                       learn from their mistakes.
